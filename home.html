The situation

The wealth of this coast is being dragged away

More than half of BC's assessed fish stocks are in trouble. Industrial draggers tow nets across nursery grounds, open-net pens still sit on wild salmon migration routes, and the push to bring crude supertankers to our North Coast keeps resurfacing. The people who pay are the people who live and work here.

Get draggers off the breadbaskets

Nine factory trawlers have swept an area of BC seafloor bigger than Ireland — through the nursery, spawning, and feeding grounds our fisheries depend on. The MPA Network puts the breadbaskets off-limits to bottom trawling.

Finish the fish-farm transition

Where open-net pens have come out of migration routes, coastal Nations and researchers are reporting stronger wild salmon returns. Finish the job — on schedule, with no more extensions.

Hold the line on tankers

The North Coast tanker ban keeps crude supertankers out of Hecate Strait and Dixon Entrance. In July 2026, Canada and BC pledged to uphold it — a win built on 50 years of coastal advocacy. The pressure to reopen it hasn't gone away. We keep watch.

The fix

Protect the engine. Abundance multiplies.

The Great Bear Sea Marine Protected Area Network shields fish nurseries, spawning grounds, and feeding grounds across 30 percent of the Northern Shelf Bioregion — planned over a decade by First Nations, the Province of BC, and Canada, with harvesters and coastal communities at the table.

Protected cores don't just guard what's inside the line. Fish grow bigger, stocks rebuild, and the surplus flows out into the fishing grounds next door. Studies measure catch rates up to 245% higher beside well-managed MPAs. That's not charity for nature — that's compound interest on the coast's savings account.
